# Source and Evidence Gate

Use for source conflicts, missing evidence, and research boundaries. Ordinary copyediting does not require this reference.

## Interpret sources by their role

- The user's request defines the task and authorization boundaries.
- Specifications, rubrics, and required templates define assessed requirements and structure.
- Relevant lecture, tutorial, lab, and applied materials establish taught methods and terminology.
- Code, data, screenshots, calculations, and recorded results support implementation and outcome claims.
- Approved outside sources support external facts; they do not create course requirements.

A rubric cannot prove an experimental result, and code inspection cannot establish a successful run. Identify which source supports each consequential claim. If sources conflict materially, explain the conflict and seek clarification instead of silently choosing.

## Handle material gaps

A gap is material when different answers could change an important fact, calculation, method, interpretation, recommendation, or assessed requirement.

Check relevant files already in scope. Ask only for missing evidence, explaining which claim depends on it. Batch related questions and pause the affected argument; continue independent work when useful. For style edits, flag an unsupported factual claim rather than expanding it or treating it as verified.

Explicitly authorized assumptions must be labelled and kept separate from observations, with their effect on conclusions explained.

## Research boundaries

Use existing research authorization without asking again. Otherwise, identify the specific evidence gap and ask before searching externally. Respect restrictions on sources and scope. Prefer relevant primary sources and cite them accurately.

## Evidence tracking

For major claims, track the source, supporting passage or value, claim status, and unresolved gap. Keep this planning internal unless a table or explanation would help the user assess the work.
