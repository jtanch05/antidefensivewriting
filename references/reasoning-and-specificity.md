# Reasoning and Specificity

Use to repair analytical gaps, not as a mandatory paragraph template.

## Find the missing link

For consequential claims, identify the assertion, supporting evidence, explanation, and implication or decision. Include conditions or limitations when they change the interpretation. Supply only the links the reader needs.

Ask:

- What specifically changed, and compared with what?
- Why does the evidence support this interpretation? Is a mechanism demonstrated or merely plausible?
- What follows for the system, stakeholder, or decision?
- Does a recommendation identify an action and responsible party where relevant?

For quantitative claims, preserve the metric, units, population or workload, timeframe, denominator, and baseline where material. A component-level improvement does not establish a whole-system improvement. Claim statistical significance only when supported by the analysis.

Illustrative repair: replace "the system is faster" with a supported statement naming the measured operation, comparator, workload, and size of the improvement. Do not invent those details to complete the sentence.

## Calibrate certainty

- **Observed:** directly shown in the evidence.
- **Calculated:** derived transparently from supplied values.
- **Inferred:** interpreted from evidence, with the basis stated.
- **Proposed:** a future action or target.
- **Unknown:** not established.

Keep these distinctions in the wording. Correlation does not establish cause; successful tested cases do not prove correctness in every case. Narrow an unsupported conclusion or use the evidence gate when the missing link is material.

## Report the repair

Identify the consequential claim, missing link, effect on the argument, and smallest useful correction. Classify factual error, unsupported inference, incomplete reasoning, vague wording, and optional polish separately. Do not turn a minor preference into a correctness failure.
